01

Executive summary

Coinscious at a glance

A personal and shared expense manager built on one honest ledger, with AI that suggests and explains but never decides money.

Coinscious records what you spend and what you share in one ledger, so your budget shows your real share, your group knows exactly who owes whom, and AI helps without ever touching the maths.

The problem

Students, flatmates and families manage personal purchases, shared bills, repayments and budgets with separate apps, spreadsheets or mental arithmetic. When one person pays a ₹3,000 grocery bill for three, a typical budget tracker counts ₹3,000 against them, a typical splitter records ₹2,000 owed, and neither shows that their real spending was ₹1,000. Typing every bill by hand is slow and full of mistakes.

The solution

Coinscious keeps one ledger in which every expense is stored as who paid and what each person's share is. Personal spending, budgets, charts, group balances and settlements are all read from those same records, so they always agree. Bills can be photographed, categories are suggested, and an assistant answers questions with figures the backend calculated, never figures the AI made up.

Central objectiveOne correct ledger: every rupee is counted once, as cash paid, as someone's share, or as a debt being settled, so personal budgets and group balances always agree.

What success looks like

Six product goals and sixteen measurable targets, each tied to a requirement and a test.

How Coinscious differs from a splitter and a budget tracker used separately

Splitting apps already offer groups, unequal splits, balances, receipts and charts, so none of those is new on its own. The difference Coinscious is built around is that one set of records feeds both the group's balances and each person's own budget.

Scope

What is in, what is out

A focused product: personal and shared expense management with honest AI help, delivered as an Android app and a website from one React Native codebase.

In scope

  • Personal expenses: add, edit, delete with undo, recent list, search and filter, recurring expenses
  • Groups for friends, flats, trips and families, with invitations, roles and leaving
  • Shared expenses with equal, exact, percent and parts splits; balances; full and partial settlements
  • Spending over time, budgets with alerts, AI-assisted budget goals
  • Bill photos turned into reviewed drafts, category suggestion, and a plain-language assistant
  • An English Android app and responsive website, in Indian rupees
  • Built and run only on free tools and free service tiers

Out of scope (future work)

  • Linking bank accounts, reading SMS or importing statements
  • Making or receiving payments (UPI collect, payment links)
  • Investment, loan, credit or tax advice
  • Multiple currencies
  • An iOS app
  • Item-by-item splitting of a bill between people
  • Any action taken without the user's confirmation

Assumptions

  • Ten members at about 8 hours a week each, roughly 80 hours a week in total; planning capacity is 55 points per one-week sprint, reset after Sprint 1 from the points actually finished.
  • Story points are the backlog's task-estimate units; the five new stories are estimated on the same scale.
  • Free tiers stay broadly available; each outside service sits behind one interface so a retired tier can be replaced by configuration.
  • Live use stays at class scale: a few dozen people a day, a few hundred bills a month.

Constraints

  • No money is spent. Every service runs on a free tier.
  • The course requires SCRUM, GitHub for all development (each commit by the person who did the work) and Slack for team communication.
  • Fixed release date: Monday 30 November 2026.
  • Personal or financial data never goes to a free tier that may train on prompts.
  • Free hosting sleeps after 15 minutes idle and has 512 MB of memory, so heavy models cannot run on the server.

Product flow

How an expense moves through the ledger

Fourteen steps in five stages, one ledger behind every number, and four rules applied everywhere.

An expense can be personal or shared, typed or scanned. Whatever the route, it ends as one database transaction that writes who paid and who owes what; everything the user sees afterwards is calculated from those records.

The ledger, worked through

Net position = what you paid + repayments you made − your shares − repayments you received. A positive number means others owe you. Within a group, positions always add up to zero.

StepEventAlex cash paid (₹)Alex spending, his share (₹)Alex net (₹)Blair net (₹)Casey net (₹)
1Alex pays ₹3,000 for groceries, split equally by three3,000.001,000.00+2,000.00−1,000.00−1,000.00
2Blair repays Alex ₹1,0003,000.001,000.00+1,000.000.00−1,000.00
3Alex adds a personal ₹500 metro recharge3,500.001,500.00+1,000.000.00−1,000.00
4Casey pays ₹100 for chai, split equally by three3,500.001,533.34+966.66−33.33−933.33

Step 4 shows the rounding rule: ₹100 becomes 10,000 paise; 10,000 ÷ 3 leaves one paisa over, which goes to the first member in the group's fixed order (Alex), so the shares are ₹33.34, ₹33.33 and ₹33.33 and still total exactly ₹100. Alex's spending rises by ₹33.34; his cash paid does not change because Casey paid.

Four rules that hold everywhere

Code does the maths; AI only suggests and explains.

Splits, balances, totals, budgets and thresholds are plain, tested code on whole paise.

Every rupee is counted once.

Your spending is your share. What you fronted for others is money owed to you. A repayment is never spending or income.

AI and OCR output is a draft until you confirm.

Nothing a model produces becomes a record without the user checking it.

Every change is atomic and recorded.

One transaction or nothing; group changes keep who, what and when.

States every screen must handle

  • Split does not add up: the amount left is shown in red and Save is disabled
  • Unsure bill field: highlighted with 'Check this', never silently saved
  • AI unavailable: categories from the local model, assistant offers example questions, bill opens as manual entry
  • Server waking up: a 'starting up, about a minute' message instead of a blank screen

System design

Architecture, contracts and diagrams

One app (Android and web), one API, one database: every request authenticated, checked against policy, and written in one transaction.

The system is one app (Android and web), one API and one database. Every request passes the same three gates in the same order: authentication, the policy check on the record, then a service that does the work inside one database transaction. AI and OCR sit behind their own interfaces and can be switched off without touching money code.

The build method

1Ledger first

The money rules are written as a document and as passing tests in Sprint 0, before any screen exists.

2One contract per endpoint

Pydantic models define every request and response; OpenAPI generates the TypeScript client, so frontend and backend cannot drift.

3Thin routes, rich services

Routes only authenticate, check policy and call a service; business rules live in services, arithmetic in the ledger engine.

4Policy before data

Every record is loaded through the policy module, which returns the record or a 404.

5Baseline kept as fallback

Every AI feature starts as rules or manual entry; the rule path stays in the code as its fallback.

6Measure before and after

Each AI story records its baseline number first, then the improved one, in the evaluation report.

Coinscious has four AI components and one guard that checks their output. Each starts from a simple baseline so the evaluation can show a before-and-after number, and the baseline is never deleted: it stays in the code as the fallback. What stays plain code

Splits, rounding, balances, settlements, totals, budget thresholds, alerts, recurring schedules, search and date ranges use no AI. These are the parts that decide money, so they must be exact, repeatable and unit-tested.

How models are run

  • The category classifier is trained on a laptop, saved with joblib (under 5 MB) and loaded once per process; it fits comfortably in the free server's 512 MB.
  • Groq models are named in config/models.yaml with their context limits; two are configured so a retired or rate-limited model can be skipped by configuration.
  • Every AI call goes through the gateway, which logs task, model, tokens, time and outcome, but never the prompt text.
  • Test sets are frozen before tuning, and labels are double-checked on a sample (agreement of 90% or more) before they are used.

AI gateway and budget

One gateway, no-training providers, zero cost

How every AI call is minimised, validated and routed, and how free tiers cover the whole project.

Every AI call goes through one gateway. It removes what the task does not need, checks the provider's circuit breaker and the daily budget, fits the input to the model's window, validates the output against a schema, retries invalid output once, moves to the next configured model on failure, and logs every attempt. When nothing answers, the calling feature runs its own fallback.

A typical active user scans one bill, asks two questions and triggers about three low-confidence category calls a day: roughly 10,000 tokens. Most category suggestions never reach the AI because the user's history and the local model answer first. At class scale this stays well inside Groq's free limits, and OCR.space's 500 a day per address is about twenty times the expected bill volume.

  • Only data the task needs is sent: descriptions or bill text, never names, emails or IDs; the assistant sends the question first and only aggregates afterwards.
  • No personal or financial data goes to any free tier that may train on prompts; such providers are not configured at all.
  • Category answers are cached per normalised merchant; bill drafts are cached by image hash so a retry does not re-read.
  • Tests never call live services; they use recorded responses.
  • If the daily budget runs low, the gateway stops calling AI for categories first, then for goal explanations, keeping bills and the assistant longest.

Engineering approach

Smart approaches at every step

Design decisions that keep the product exact, private and resilient.

These approaches are what make Coinscious exact and dependable on free infrastructure. Each one is enforced by a test, not by convention.

Accounts and sessions

The unique index, not a pre-check, prevents duplicate accounts. Generic login errors stop account discovery. Rotating refresh tokens per device make 'sign out everywhere' one query.

Adding an expense

Money is parsed once into paise at the edge. An idempotency key makes a double-tap or a retry harmless. Optimistic display makes the list feel instant while the server confirms.

Categories

The user's own history answers first, a 5 MB local model second, and AI only when both are unsure, so most suggestions are instant, free and private.

Bills

The app shrinks and cleans the photo, so the 1 MB OCR limit and privacy are handled before upload. Rules extract the numbers; AI only arranges items, and any number not in the OCR text is thrown away.

Splits

One allocate() function serves all four modes, with the largest-remainder rule and a fixed tie order, so the same input always gives the same paise.

Balances

Positions are computed from records, not stored running totals, so an edit can never leave a stale balance. Debt simplification is a suggestion; pairwise balances remain visible.

Settlements

A settlement is its own record that moves positions only, so repayments never inflate anyone's spending or income.

Budgets and goals

Budgets count shares. Alerts fire once per crossing through a unique key. Recommendations are a trimmed median a person can check, with AI only for wording.

Recurring

Occurrences are keyed by rule and date, and created by an hourly job plus a catch-up on login, so a sleeping server neither misses nor duplicates.

Assistant

AI picks from eight fixed tools; code resolves dates and computes; a guard rejects any sentence with a number the tools did not return.

Platform

Ledger first, policy before data, one transaction per change, typed contracts end to end, baselines kept as fallbacks, and a CI gate that measures AI quality.

The ten build rules

  1. Money is whole paise. No floats in money code, ever; formatting into ₹ happens only for display.
  2. The ledger engine is the only place shares and balances are calculated. Nothing else does arithmetic on money.
  3. One change, one transaction. Expense, payments, shares and history are written together or not at all.
  4. Check every record on the server. Load through the policy module; strangers get 404.
  5. AI output is a draft. Nothing from OCR or a model becomes a record until the user confirms.
  6. AI never computes a figure. It chooses tools and writes sentences; every number it shows comes from the backend.
  7. Untrusted text is data. Bill text, descriptions and questions are wrapped as data in prompts, and output must match a schema.
  8. Every outside call has a timeout and a fallback. OCR, AI or email can never stop a save or break a screen.
  9. Every retry is safe. Creates carry an idempotency key; occurrences and notifications have unique keys.
  10. Migrations only. The schema changes through reviewed Alembic files, never by hand in a dashboard.

Glossary

Plain-English meanings of the terms used in this document.

Allocated share
The part of an expense assigned to one person; what counts as their spending.
Audit event
A history record of who changed what and when, with before and after values.
Baseline
The simple first version of a component, kept as its fallback and used to measure improvement.
Cash paid
Money a person actually paid out, including what they fronted for others.
Circuit breaker
Stops calling a failing service for a cool-down, then tries again.
Debt simplification
Turning many pairwise debts into the fewest repayments with the same result.
Draft
An unsaved expense produced from a bill; it becomes a record only when confirmed.
Grounding guard
A check that every number in AI-written text came from the backend's figures.
Idempotency key
A unique key on a request so repeating it never creates a second record.
Largest-remainder rule
Rounding each share down to the paisa, then giving leftover paise one by one in a fixed order.
Macro-F1
A quality score averaged equally over every category, so rare ones count as much as common ones.
Net position
What a member paid plus repayments made, minus their shares and repayments received; positive means others owe them.
OCR
Optical character recognition: reading text from an image.
Paise
One hundredth of a rupee; every amount is stored as a whole number of paise.
Policy module
The single place that decides whether a user may see or change a record.
Property-based test
A test that checks a rule on thousands of generated inputs instead of a few examples.
Settlement
A repayment between two group members; it reduces debt and never counts as spending.
Soft delete
Marking a record deleted so it can be undone, before it is purged later.
TF-IDF
A way of turning text into numbers that weights words by how distinctive they are.
Version (optimistic concurrency)
A counter on each expense that blocks one person's save from silently overwriting another's.
